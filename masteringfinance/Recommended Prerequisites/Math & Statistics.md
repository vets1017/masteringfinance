# Math & Statistics

Finance requires math. Mathematics is the quantitative backbone of finance. While basic arithmetic handles your personal budget; probability and statistics are required to measure risk, forecast future growth, and optimize an investment portfolio.

A lot of people usually get intimidated by the math in finance, unless you are breaking into Quantitative Finance, you don't need a PhD in theoretical physics. A basic solid understanding of algebra, probability, and statistics should do you good.

--- 
## Core
### Refresher
If you are not confident in your mathematical skills or just need a quick refresher, you can try these courses.

**Algebra 1** */ Khan Academy* [Course](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/algebra)
- This free course covers algebraic expressions, solving equations and inequalities, linear functions, exponents, polynomials, and quadratics. 
**Statistics and Probability** */ Khan Academy* [Course](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/statistics-probability)
- Finance is all about measuring risk, and risk is simply just probability. This free course covers the foundations of collecting, analyzing, and determining conclusions from data. 

### Probability & Statistics

**MitX: Probability - The Science of uncertainty and Data** */ MIT, edX* [Course](https://www.edx.org/learn/probability/massachusetts-institute-of-technology-probability-the-science-of-uncertainty-and-data)
- Builds foundation in probability, random variables, and stochastic processes. Useful for quantitative modeling and risk analysis. Plus, this course is entirely free!

### Calculus

**Essence of Calculus** */ 3Blue1Brown, YouTube* [Playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLZHQObOWTQDMsr9K-rj53DwVRMYO3t5Yr)
- "A free course offering the core concept of Calculus, with a visuals-first approach." Calculus is used in pricing options and derivatives. This playlist by 3Blue1Brown teaches Calculus and its many fundamental topics through visuals.
**Single Variable Calculus** */ MIT OpenCourseWare, Fall 2010* [Course](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/)
- Free university-level rigor provided by MIT's OpenCourseWare program. 
**Applied Calculus for Business, Economics, and the Social and Life Sciences, Expanded Edition** */ Laurence Hoffmann, Gerald Bradley*, 2009 [Book ~$107]
- A highly recommended textbook created specifically for business applications.

### Advanced

**Differential Equations** */ MIT OpenCourseWare, Spring 2010* [Course](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-03-differential-equations-spring-2010/)
- Very useful if you are planning to dive deep into quantitative finance or algorithmic trading.
**Regression Models** */ Johns Hopkins University, Coursera* [Course](https://www.coursera.org/learn/regression-models)
- Essential for understanding how different financial variables (e.g. interest rates, stock prices) correlates with one another.

## Supplementary

**Probability Theory: The Logic of Science** */ E.T. Jaynes*
- Recommended for individuals who already have a strong background in probability and want to master the theory. 
- [Free Manuscript Hosted by McGill University's Biostatistics department](https://jhanley.biostat.mcgill.ca/bios601/GaussianModel/JaynesProbabilityTheory.pdf)
- [Hardcover ~$92](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0521592712)
**Statistics in a Nutshell: A Desktop Quick Reference** */ Sarah Boslaugh, 2012* [Book ~$17](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1449316824)
- Useful for a quick, practical reference
**Probability with Martingales** */ David Williams, 1991* [Book ~$39](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0521406056?lv=shuf&channelId=500&plpRedirect=mhFallback)
- Introduction to the more modern and rigorous side in probability. Williams connects classical probability and advanced measure theory with the use of discrete-time martingales. A very useful textbook for those who are serious about probability, statistics, and mathematical finance.
